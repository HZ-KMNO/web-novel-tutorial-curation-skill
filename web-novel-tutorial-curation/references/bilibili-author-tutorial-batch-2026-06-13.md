## Topic Classification

### Platform Pragmatism And Market Freshness

Key source: `343758595-1-208.mp4_原文.docx`.

Transferable lessons:

- Online fiction is not rewarded simply because the author worked for years; execution, market fit, update discipline, and current reading habits matter.
- Copyright/platform choices are strategic decisions, not moral slogans. An AI curator should record the advice as market pragmatism, not universal legal or career guidance.
- Long planning without writing can become self-comfort. For serial fiction, consistent output and continuous reading of current market works keep the author from aging out of reader taste.

AI usage:

- Use this source when curating author career advice, market awareness, platform pragmatism, and update discipline.
- Do not convert personal contract anecdotes into legal advice.
- What an AI writer can do with this: ask whether the project is producing readable chapters and learning from current readers, not only polishing an imagined masterpiece.

### Outline As Story Planning, Not Formula

Key source: `382938159-1-208.mp4_原文.docx`.

Transferable lessons:

- Having an outline does not automatically make a novel good; lacking a written outline does not automatically make it bad.
- The useful layer is story planning: what kind of novel this is, what the protagonist wants, where the major pressure and upgrades occur, and what the reader should expect at key ranges.
- A shallow chapter-by-chapter list is less useful than knowing when the protagonist is pressured, when the golden finger pays off, when the first major enemy appears, and when the story tier changes.
- In familiar web-novel frames, the differentiator is often protagonist appeal, detail execution, and smoothness rather than a unique macro skeleton.

AI usage:

- Use this source when a user asks for an outline, but avoid producing empty formal outlines.
- What an AI writer can do with this: write a story plan with phase goals, protagonist appeal, pressure points, upgrade/payoff points, and major enemy timing before expanding chapters.

### Opening Protagonist Appeal

Key source: `723352712-1-208.mp4_原文.docx`.

Transferable lessons:

- An opening can make readers like the protagonist through action under pressure: give the protagonist choices that reveal temperament.
- If high-level behavior-based characterization is hard, give the protagonist a concrete problem that any reader wants solved.
- If neither is ready, use a familiar identity or social group so readers can quickly locate the protagonist, but the identity must be lived-in and accurate.

AI usage:

- Use this source when auditing chapter one or the first protagonist scene.
- What an AI writer can do with this: check whether the first scene lets the protagonist act, solve, choose, or occupy a familiar identity readers can immediately understand.

### Sensory Detail, Synesthesia, And Consciousness Boundaries

Key source: `426863561-1-208.mp4_原文.docx`.

Transferable lessons:

- Five-sense writing is not decoration; it creates physical reality and lets readers feel the scene.
- Synesthesia can make perception stronger by letting sound have color, color have temperature, or pain become an object/action.
- A word bank is useful only when organized by felt experience, emotion, texture, sound, smell, or bodily response; it should not be a copied phrase dump.
- Stream-of-consciousness writing must still have theme, emotional logic, and viewpoint pressure. It is not random fragmentation.

AI usage:

- Use this source when curating description, atmosphere, horror, romance, injury, memory, or consciousness scenes.
- What an AI writer can do with this: add 2-3 relevant sensory anchors tied to POV and emotion, then remove generic adjectives.

### BE, Pain Points, And Emotional Knives

Key source: `459105250-1-208.mp4_原文.docx`.

Transferable lessons:

- Killing a character is not automatically the strongest pain. Unavoidable consequences, missed timing, unspoken love, shame, duty, and moral contradiction can hurt more.
- Pain works after attachment. Give readers joy, routine, private sweetness, and character charm before breaking something.
- A good "knife" is logically inevitable from character desire, flaw, family, duty, identity, or outside pressure.
- Use action, behavior, memory, scenery, and third-party reaction to carry pain. Excessive direct psychological explanation can limit reader empathy.

AI usage:

- Use this source for romance, tragedy, sacrifice, blackening, team-break, and moral-dilemma scenes.
- What an AI writer can do with this: before writing pain, list the desire, the wrong recognition, the delayed action, the missed chance, and the changed relationship.

### Combat Scene Clarity And Type-Specific Fight Design

Key source: `155292300-1-208.mp4_原文.docx`.

Transferable lessons:

- Fight scenes vary by genre. Urban fights often need fast payoff; fantasy fights can sustain exchange and spectacle; supernatural and detective fights often rely more on rules, tools, chase, trap, and inference than pure blows.
- New writers should keep combat clear: who attacks, who defends, what move is used, what changes, and who wins the exchange.
- Environment can show power scale more clearly than injury adjectives: cracked floor, broken door, stalled elevator, shattering glass, extinguished lights, blocked exit.
- A fight is satisfying only when setup has raised emotional pressure. Without setup, action reads like filler.

AI usage:

- Use this source when curating action scenes, supernatural chase scenes, urban confrontation, or boss fights.
- What an AI writer can do with this: write combat as setup -> attack -> response -> environmental proof -> result -> new pressure, with genre-specific length.

### Golden Three Chapters

Key source: `146029883-1-208.mp4_原文.docx`.

Transferable lessons:

- In the first 2000-3000 words, readers should know the protagonist, what happens to them, what consequence follows, and what small suspense pulls them into the next chapter.
- Do not crowd chapter one with world history, family trees, class rosters, or group introductions.
- Start while the event is happening, not long before or after it.
- Chapter endings should stop at a live conflict or unresolved small question, not a flat summary.

AI usage:

- Use this source for opening audits, Qidian-facing first chapters, and first-three-chapter plans.
- What an AI writer can do with this: check whether the first chapter contains protagonist focus, event-in-progress, consequence, and hook before adding background.

### AI Prose De-Flavoring And Human Taste

Key source: `38657460639-1-192.mp4_原文.docx`.

Transferable lessons:

- Removing AI flavor is only a baseline; the story still needs strong plot, character, and reader pull.
- Common AI-flavor markers include unnecessary quotation marks for emphasis, odd foreign-coded setting compounds, uncanny noun/adjective combinations, excessive metaphors, and "fictional completion" where the model bridges missing logic as if it happened.
- Prompting and revision should frame the story for Chinese online-fiction readers: domestic context, familiar social texture, legible objects, and natural wording.
- Human taste must judge whether the prose feels like a person with lived context wrote it.

AI usage:

- Use this source when curating de-AI checks, final polish workflows, or prompt constraints.
- What an AI writer can do with this: scan final prose for quote abuse, uncanny collocations, foreign-coded setting drift, over-metaphor, and missing causal steps silently patched by narration.
